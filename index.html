How LSAC Calculates GPA

The Law School Admission Council (LSAC) recalculates undergraduate academic records through the Credential Assembly Service (CAS) using a standardized 4.33 GPA scale.

This process helps law schools compare applicants fairly across different colleges, universities, and grading systems.

What LSAC Includes in GPA Calculation

  • All undergraduate coursework completed before earning your first bachelor’s degree
  • Courses taken at multiple colleges or universities
  • Repeated courses and retaken classes
  • Punitive withdrawals and failing grades
  • Credits earned through summer or extension programs

What LSAC May Exclude

  • Non-punitive withdrawals
  • Pass/fail courses without grade values
  • Graduate or professional school coursework
  • Audited classes without academic credit

Frequently Asked Questions

What is LSAC GPA?

LSAC GPA is a standardized GPA calculated by the Law School Admission Council to compare applicants fairly across different institutions.

How is it different from my university GPA?

It includes all undergraduate coursework, applies a standardized grading scale, and may count repeated courses differently.

Does LSAC include repeated or failed courses?

Yes. LSAC typically includes all attempts, which can impact your final GPA.

Is this calculator accurate?

Yes. It follows LSAC grading rules and provides a reliable estimate of your official LSAC GPA.
